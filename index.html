How to Read

One Figure, One Argument

This is a single unified figure. The three theoretical frameworks are not parallel lenses sitting side by side — they operate at different logical depths within the same causal architecture.

  • GSTdefines the state space: the structural facts, actors, and system conditions that determine what is possible
  • Papplies normative constraints within that state space — what is ethically permissible given what is structurally real
  • DJevaluates the output — whether the result is equitable, and if not, which upstream condition requires reform

The four data-sharing stages (Generation, Processing/Storage, Access/Sharing, Use/Translation) are the empirical object that GST maps. Each stage is then assessed by Principlism and evaluated by Distributive Justice before the pipeline advances.

GST — Systemic Frame

Framing Actors and Relationships

General Systems Theory is the foundational, exogenous layer — the structural facts of the world before any ethical principle is applied. Its function here is not descriptive but architecturally necessary: it defines the state space within which ethical decisions must operate.

GST operates across four simultaneous levels: individual (participant genetics, values, age, gender, community ties), institutional (biobanks, research sites, RECs), national (Ugandan law and policy), and international (H3Africa, EGA, dbGaP). These levels are not a hierarchy to be climbed — they are co-present at every stage of the pipeline.

The Stakeholder Network node — participants, researchers, national RECs, funders, lawyers, CAB members, publishers, regulators, and collaborators — sits at the intersection of all four levels. Lawyers are named explicitly because data-sharing disputes are resolved in legal systems, not ethics committees alone.

No good intention overcomes absent infrastructure. Ethical possibility is bounded by systemic reality.

The Governing Framework node captures the boundary conditions: the institutional, national, and international guidelines, policies, and laws within which all four stages of data sharing must operate.

Principlism — Ethical Quality

Assessing Ethical Quality at Each Stage

Principlism (Beauchamp & Childress) functions here not as aspirational ideals but as hard constraints on permissible action within the state space defined upstream. Autonomy, Beneficence, Non-maleficence, and Justice do not float free — they operate inside empirical limits set by the GST layer.

Crucially, the figure shows Principlism applied at each stage, not once at the start. The ethical questions differ by stage:

  • S1Autonomy in consent; Non-maleficence at point of collection
  • S2Beneficence in storage design; Autonomy in secondary-use governance
  • S3Justice in access equity; Non-maleficence via stigmatisation protections
  • S4Beneficence in results return; Justice in authorship and attribution

Social and relational risks — stigmatisation, discrimination, shame — appear at Stage 3 as structural variables to be modelled, not external risks to be noted in passing. Frameworks that omit them produce ethics that are aspirational but empirically inert in African contexts.

Distributive Justice — Equity

Evaluating Equity at Each Stage

Distributive Justice is the diagnostic endpoint — the measurable test of whether the system produces just outcomes. Applied at each stage, it shifts from a conclusion appended to the framework into an ongoing audit woven through every relationship.

  • S1Who bears the burden of participation? Coercion risk in LMIC recruitment
  • S2Data sovereignty: who controls the repository? Infrastructure gap as injustice
  • S3Ethical imperialism: do HIC institutions gatekeep access to African data?
  • S4Does Uganda derive gains from its own data? Legal sovereignty at translation

The feedback arrow on the right margin is the framework's diagnostic mechanism: when outcomes are inequitable, the error is identified and traced back to the specific upstream GST condition that must be reconfigured. This is what makes the framework actionable rather than descriptive.

Power Dynamics and Legal & Sovereignty Issues are co-equal analytical concerns, not footnotes. Commercialisation, intellectual property, and national sovereignty are substantive equity questions addressed structurally by the framework.

Core Axiom

The Thesis in One Sentence

"If outcomes are inequitable, the error is upstream."

Ethics is not an abstract overlay — it is a function of state space. Systemic capacity dictates what is ethically possible. When inequity appears at the output, the corrective action must begin at the level of General Systems Theory: examining and adjusting the structural conditions that made those outcomes probable.

Moral exhortation addressed at individual actors cannot substitute for structural redesign. This is the framework's core contribution: not a new set of principles, but a new architecture for understanding why existing principles so often fail to produce just outcomes in practice in African and LMIC genomic research contexts.

The three frameworks are unified because they operate at different logical levels of the same argument — GST provides the constraints, Principlism applies the norms within those constraints, and Distributive Justice measures whether the system-level result is just. None of the three can function without the other two.
